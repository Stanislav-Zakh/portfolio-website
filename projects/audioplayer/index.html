Modern Audio Player with WPF and .NET 8

The Audio Player was built using WPF and .NET 8. As WPF strongly encourages the use of the MVVM architectural pattern, I implemented this pattern to separate the application's business and presentation logic from its user interface. By following this approach, I created a more maintainable and scalable application. To enhance playback and audio processing, I integrated the powerful NAudio library, which played a crucial role in delivering a seamless listening experience.

Metadata Editing & Image Gallery

“Neon Lights” by

Blear

While the player includes all standard features like playlists, track selection, and playback controls, I wanted to take it a step further. One standout feature is the ability to read and edit metadata tags directly within the player - even while a track is playing. Using the reliable TagLibSharp library, users can modify track details such as the title and artist in real-time. Additionally, because music is often tied to visual experiences, I introduced an Image Gallery that allows users to add and display images or GIFs that match their music’s mood. Since WPF struggles with GIF rendering, I utilized the XAMLAnimatedGif library to ensure smooth playback.

What’s Next? Future Enhancements

“Corporations” by

Kenze Wee

Currently, I’m working on a 3D visualizer using OpenGL, which I plan to integrate into the player as an additional page, similar to the Image Gallery. Another upcoming feature is a dedicated Playlist Library page, designed to offer more advanced playlist and track management. These enhancements will further elevate the user experience, making the player more immersive and customizable.

The Audio Player does not come with any preloaded images or GIFs. The pretty artworks seen in the displayed images are solely for demonstrating the functionality of the image gallery.
